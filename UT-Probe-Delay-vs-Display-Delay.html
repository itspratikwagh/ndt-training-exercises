    Hint

    Key points

    Epoch: ZERO OFFSET · µs

    Probe delay moves zero

    • Removes the time sound spends in the wear plate, delay line and couplant, so zero is the test surface.
    • Changes every reading by the same amount.
    • Always used. Set it in every calibration (CAL ZERO on the thin step, CAL VEL on the thick step).
    • Recheck when you change the probe, cable, delay line or couplant, and as the wear face wears.
    Epoch: DELAY · mm

    Display delay moves the window

    • Slides the screen's left edge deeper into the part, like panning a camera.
    • Readings do not change. Only what you can see changes.
    • Used when needed: zooming into a depth band on thick parts, pushing the water path off-screen in immersion, spreading out the region near the back wall.
    • Set it back to 0 when you're done, or near-surface flaws on the next job will be off-screen.
    Immersion

    Both controls can handle the water path

    • At steel velocity, water looks about 4× longer than it is: 50 mm of water shows up as about 200 mm.
    • DELAY pushes the water path off-screen, but readings are still measured from the probe and include the water.
    • ZERO OFFSET can absorb the water time, like a very long delay line. Depths then read from the front surface. Calibrating on a reference block in the tank at the same water path does this for you.
    • It only holds while the water path is fixed: 1 mm more water shifts every reading about 4 mm in steel. Re-standardize, or use an interface gate if your instrument has one.
    • Setup check: water path ≥ ¼ × steel thickness, so the 2nd interface echo lands beyond the back wall.
