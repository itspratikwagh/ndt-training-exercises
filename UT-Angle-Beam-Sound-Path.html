Angle Beam Sound Path: Where Does the Sound Go?

← Exercise Hub

An angle-beam probe sends sound into the steel at an angle. Follow the sound through a 1 inch plate, see why a plain plate gives no echo, compare the skip for three wedges, and find the one place the plate sends the sound back.

Wedge Move probe
Drag the probe along the plate (or click the plate first and use ← →). Change the wedge with the buttons. The probe stays the same; only the wedge changes the angle.
Steps
    ✓ Exercise complete. Sound only comes back from something that faces the beam.

    Key points

    • The angle comes from the wedge. A 45° wedge sends the sound into the steel at 45°; the probe on top can be the same for every wedge.
    • Leg 1 runs from the top surface down to the back wall. Leg 2 runs from the back wall back up to the top surface. Where the sound reaches the top surface again is one skip.
    • In a 1 inch plate, one skip is 2.000″ with a 45° wedge, 3.464″ with a 60° wedge and 5.495″ with a 70° wedge. The bigger the angle, the flatter the sound travels and the longer the skip.
    • In a plate with smooth surfaces and no flaws, the sound zig-zags away from the probe and never comes back: no echo.
    • A corner (back wall meeting the end of the plate) sends the sound straight back to the probe. That's a corner trap. You can catch it with leg 1 or, farther back, with leg 2.
