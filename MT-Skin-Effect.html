The Concept

When alternating current flows through a conductor, the changing magnetic field induces opposing eddy currents that are strongest at the center of the part. The result: current — and the magnetic flux it produces — crowds toward the surface. This is the skin effect. In MT it means AC magnetization concentrates the field where surface defects live, while direct current penetrates the full cross-section and can reveal subsurface defects. The higher the frequency, the thinner the "skin."

δ = 1 / √(π · f · μ · σ)   — standard depth of penetration (J drops to ≈37% of surface value)
