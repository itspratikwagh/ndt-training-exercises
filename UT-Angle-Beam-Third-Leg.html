One-Sided Access: Find the Far Side with Leg 3

← Exercise Hub

A stiffener next to this weld means you can only scan from one side. With a 30° bevel and a 60° wedge, legs 1 and 2 can't reach the top of the far-side fusion face, but leg 3 can, as long as the A-scan range is long enough to show it.

60° wedge · 30° bevel
Drag the probe along the plate. Change the A-scan range with the slider.
Steps
    ✓ Exercise complete. One-sided access: increase the range and use leg 3.

    Key points

    • Sometimes you can only scan from one side of a weld: a stiffener, flange, nozzle or other structure is in the way.
    • With a 30° bevel and a 60° wedge, leg 1 meets only the lower part of the far-side fusion face. To reach higher, the probe would have to sit on the weld.
    • Leg 3 runs the same way as leg 1 (down at 60°) after leg 2 bounces off the top surface, so it meets the upper far-side face square-on. The cap has to be ground flush for that bounce.
    • One skip (leg 1 + leg 2) at 60° in 1 inch plate is 4.000″ of sound path. A leg 3 echo comes after that, so set the range past one skip or the echo is off the screen.
    • On leg 3 the sound is heading down from the top again: depth = sound path × cos 60° − 2 × thickness. The instrument's leg and depth readouts do this for you.
