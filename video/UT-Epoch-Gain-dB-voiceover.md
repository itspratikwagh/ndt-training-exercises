# Voice-over script: Gain and decibels: the 6 dB rule

For `UT-Epoch-Gain-dB.html` and its MP4 (1920×1080, 2:01). The subtitles in the video and in `UT-Epoch-Gain-dB.srt` use exactly these lines.

**How to record**
- Read each line starting at its timecode. Each line is timed for a steady pace of about 150 words per minute, and the on-screen action (key presses, knob turns) happens during the line.
- If you stretch a line, slow the clip slightly in your editor, or leave a small pause before the next line.
- 15 lines, 288 words in total.

| Time | Line |
|---|---|
| `0:00` | Gain controls how tall echoes look on the screen. It is measured in decibels, or dB, and it never moves an echo left or right. |
| `0:10` | The Epoch is calibrated, the probe is on the 0.300 inch step, and Gate 1 is on the first back-wall echo. |
| `0:19` | First, bring the echo to a standard height. Press 2nd F, then GATES. That is AUTO XX percent. |
| `0:26` | The Epoch sets the gain so the gated echo sits at 80 percent of full screen height. That gain, 42.5 dB, is our reference. |
| `0:36` | Press dB so the knob controls gain. The delta in the gain box shows the step: 1 dB per click. |
| `0:44` | Press the check key until the step reads 6 dB. |
| `0:48` | Every 6 dB is a factor of two. Take away 6 dB: one click to the left. |
| `0:55` | The echo drops from 80 to 40 percent. Half the height. |
| `1:00` | Another 6 dB down, 12 dB in total. Now it is at 20 percent, one quarter of where it started. |
| `1:08` | Two clicks to the right brings us back to the reference gain, and the echo is back at 80 percent. |
| `1:17` | Now add 6 dB above the reference. The echo should double, to 160 percent. |
| `1:22` | But the screen only goes to 100 percent, so the echo runs off the top. The amplitude box just says more than 110 percent. It is really at 160. |
| `1:34` | An echo off the top of the screen is saturated. You cannot measure its height, so bring the gain back down before you compare echoes. |
| `1:44` | Remember: 6 dB doubles or halves the height. 12 dB is four times. 20 dB is ten times. |
| `1:52` | Now try it yourself. Predict each height first, then turn the knob and check your answer. |
